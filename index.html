What building in Detroit demands of a firm

Detroit's defining engineering skill is respect for existing structure. Heavy-timber and early-steel mill buildings, masonry shells that have carried a hundred winters of freeze-thaw, flat roofs under Great Lakes snow — adaptive reuse done right starts with reading the building that is actually there, not the one on the old sheet. The new work is just as demanding: EV and battery plants, supplier facilities, and logistics across the metro run on heavy floor loads, process utilities, and fast schedules. The firms that serve this market well carry structure, systems, and civil inside one roof, so an industrial conversion or a new plant building gets one accountable department instead of three firms exchanging assumptions. Michigan professional engineers are licensed through the Board of Professional Engineers in the Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs (LARA), and the engineer who seals a Michigan set has to hold that license — a check any buyer can run before signing anything.

How do I verify an engineer is licensed in Michigan?

Michigan licenses professional engineers through the Board of Professional Engineers, part of the Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs (LARA), which maintains the state's public license verification. Before you hire, confirm the engineer in responsible charge holds an active Michigan license in the discipline your project needs — structural, mechanical, electrical, or civil — and that the firm is authorized to offer engineering in the state. Licensure and coverage is the first of the six criteria this ranking scores.
